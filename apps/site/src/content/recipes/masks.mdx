The viewport clips the reels through a `MaskStrategy`, so buffer cells never
leak past the visible grid. Two strategies have always shipped and both draw
hard rectangles: `RectMaskStrategy` (one rect per reel, the default) and
`SharedRectMaskStrategy` (a single bounding box).

Everything on this page is additive. Existing sets are unaffected.

## Which one do I want?

| Board | Strategy |
|---|---|
| Square frame art | `RectMaskStrategy` (default) - nothing to do |
| Rounded frame art, reels flush | `RoundedRectMaskStrategy` (`scope: 'set'`) |
| Reels drawn as separate cards, with a cross gap | `RoundedRectMaskStrategy` (`scope: 'reel'`) |
| Pyramid / MultiWays, rounded, reels flush | `SilhouetteMaskStrategy` |
| Anything else - hexes, holes, cut-outs | `PathMaskStrategy` |

Two decorators wrap any of them, yours included: `inset(strategy, px)` shrinks
what it draws, and `composeMasks(a, b, ...)` unions several into the viewport's
one mask.

## A rounded window

`scope: 'set'` rounds the four corners of the union bounding box. Safe at any

## Each reel as its own card

`scope: 'reel'` rounds every reel box instead.

**Gotcha:** this needs a non-zero CROSS gap (`symbolGap.x` on a vertical set,
`symbolGap.y` on a horizontal one). At gap `0` neighbouring reels share an edge,
and rounding both sides of a shared edge bites a lens-shaped notch out of every
seam. The strategy warns once to the console when it sees touching rects.

## Rounding a jagged board

A pyramid has no single rectangle to round. Per-reel rounding notches every
seam; bounding-box rounding throws the staircase away and shows buffer cells
past the short reels.

`SilhouetteMaskStrategy` walks the rectilinear union outline of the reel rects
and rounds every vertex of it - outer corners bulging out, and the inward
corners of each step curving the other way with their own `concaveRadius`. On a
uniform set the outline *is* the bounding box, so it degrades to a rounded
rectangle with no special case.

Reels must butt together (cross gap `0`); with a gap they are genuinely
disjoint, and the strategy says so once and falls back to per-reel rounded
rects.

## Any shape, without a class

`PathMaskStrategy` takes a `(graphics, context) => void`. The context carries
`rects`, `width`, `height`, `axis` and `bleed`, so an orientation-aware mask
stays a one-liner. Do not call `g.clear()` - the strategy already did.

`Graphics.cut()` subtracts a path from the last shape that was already
**filled**, which is how you punch a hole; `regularPoly` gives you hex and
diamond boards.

**Gotcha:** the fill comes first. `rect(...).fill(...)` then `rect(...).cut()`.
Draw the hole before filling and `cut()` finds nothing to cut from - you get an
empty mask and a completely invisible board, with no error.

## Insetting and composing

`inset(strategy, px)` shrinks any strategy's output uniformly - the fix for "the
art bleeds a pixel past the frame" without rewriting the strategy. Negative
grows instead.

`composeMasks(...)` unions several strategies into one mask, for a board plus a
banner or side cell that has to share the viewport's single mask.

**Union only.** A PixiJS Graphics mask is the union of every filled shape in it,
and there is no way to intersect or subtract the output of two independent
strategies. To subtract *within* one strategy, use `.cut()` inside a
`PathMaskStrategy`.

## Writing your own

`MaskStrategy` is public. Implement `build(ctx)` and `update(g, ctx)`, set
`version = MASK_STRATEGY_VERSION`, and pass it to `.maskStrategy(...)`. Reach
for `PathMaskStrategy` first - a class only earns its keep when the strategy
holds state across draws.

Two things a hand-written strategy must respect:

- **`ctx.axis`.** `rects` are screen-space in every orientation, so which of
  their four numbers runs along the strip depends on the axis.
  `axis.toLocal(width, height)` gives you the pair as `{ cross, main }`.
- **`ctx.bleed`.** Cross-axis room per side that `curveBleed(...)` asked for.
  Inflate by it on the CROSS axis only; the main axis is where the buffer cells
  live and they are meant to stay hidden.

Implement the optional `draw(g, ctx)` as well and your strategy composes with
`composeMasks` and `inset` like the built-ins do. Without it both still work -
`composeMasks` nests your Graphics as a child - at the cost of one scene node.
