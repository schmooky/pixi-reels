## One number

A slot reel is a drum. The middle cell faces you; the top and bottom ones have rotated away, so their far edge sits further from your eye. A camera turns those into **trapezoids**, not smaller rectangles.

`.curve(amount)`. `0` flat (default, free), `1` hard barrel. Most boards want `0.3`&ndash;`0.5`.

Grid lines on the tiles are the tell: they converge on the outer rows, stay parallel on the middle one.

<RecipeDemo code="curved-reels" height={420} />

`depth` is perspective strength - how much smaller an edge cell is than the middle one. `0` = orthographic: cells still bunch, nothing recedes, nothing keystones. Defaults to `amount * 0.5`.

```ts
builder.curve(0.45);                          // depth 0.225
builder.curve({ amount: 0.45, depth: 0.35 }); // stronger
```

Capped just under `cos(arc)`. Past that the projection stops being monotonic and edge cells fold back over each other, so a deep curve saturates instead of turning inside out.

## The middle cell is never deformed

The cell facing the camera is the one *not* turned away, so it is drawn at exactly its authored size - both axes, no keystone, 1:1. Everything else bends around it.

That has a consequence worth knowing: a drum whose middle is 1:1 **cannot** also fill a window sized for N flat cells. Its ends fall short, and what shows in that band is the buffer cells, compressed hard as they curve away.

That band is a feature, not a leak - it is the sliver of the next symbol you see on a real cabinet, and on a real cabinet the bezel covers it. Do the same: mask or frame the top and bottom of the reel window. `bufferSymbols(2)` gives the band more to draw if your curve is deep.

The alternative - stretching the projection to touch the window edges - magnifies the main axis at the centre while leaving the cross axis alone, so the middle row comes out visibly taller than it was authored. Not worth it.

## Two ways to draw it

Every demo on this page uses `.curveMode('warp')`. It is the one you want.

```ts
builder.curve(0.45).curveMode('warp').renderer(app.renderer);
```

**`'warp'`** renders each reel to a texture and draws it through a mesh whose **vertices** are displaced by the projection. The container bends, so everything inside it bends - Spine skeletons, atlas sprites, text, effects - and no symbol cooperates or even knows the reel is curved.

It also bends **motion**. The curve is on the rendered reel, not baked into each cell, so anything moving the strip rides it: the spin, the stop bounce, cascade falls.

**`'symbol'`** (default) projects each cell on its own. Crisper - no resample - and free, but a `Container` transform is affine, so it can only bend content that IS a texture. Spine, `Graphics`, composite subtrees and *atlas sub-frames* fall back to a uniform scale: displaced and shrunk, never keystoned. And the stop bounce translates the reel container flat, so the board slides straight up and down instead of travelling round the drum.

| | `'symbol'` | `'warp'` |
|---|---|---|
| Spine / composites bend | no | **yes** |
| Atlas art keystones | no | **yes** |
| Motion follows the curve | no | **yes** |
| Cost | free | 1 render pass per reel per frame |
| Sharpness | native | one resample |
| Needs `renderer()` | no | **yes** - `build()` throws without it |

Under `'warp'`, symbols stop being real display objects to the renderer, so use `getCellQuad()` rather than screen-space hit testing.

### Symbols that hang over their cell

Two knobs, and they compose.

`SymbolData.zIndex` stacks symbols **within** a reel. It is folded in as `zIndex * 100 + cellIndex`, so any raised value beats every cell index and that symbol draws over its neighbours whichever row it lands on. Reapplied on every wrap, so it holds mid-spin.

`.curveBleed(px)` gives the warp texture room across the strip. The warp renders each reel into a texture the size of the reel, so art wider than its cell is sliced off at the boundary; bleed captures the overhang, warps it with everything else, and lets it stick out.

```ts
builder
  .symbolData({ mystery: { zIndex: 2 } }) // draws over its neighbours
  .curveBleed(46);                        // and is allowed to hang over them
```

Pair bleed with `SharedRectMaskStrategy` - a `curveFocus` other than `'reel'` already selects it - or the per-reel mask clips the overhang straight back off. It costs texture area, so keep it to what the art needs.

## Where the camera stands

Default: one camera per reel, so every reel is its own drum. `.curveFocus('set')`: one camera on the middle of the board, so receding cells also lean IN and the grid reads as one wide cylinder.

```ts
builder.curve(0.5).curveFocus('reel');     // default - five separate drums
builder.curve(0.5).curveFocus('set-lean'); // halfway
builder.curve(0.5).curveFocus('set');      // one camera, whole board
```

Watch the outer reels tilt toward the middle while the centre one barely moves. `'set-lean'` is usually the sweet spot on a 5-wide board.

<RecipeDemo code="curved-reels-focus" height={420} />

**Gotcha:** leaning cells cross their own column, and the per-reel `RectMaskStrategy` would slice the overhang off. Anything but `'reel'` auto-selects `SharedRectMaskStrategy`. An explicit `.maskStrategy(...)` still wins.

## One drum, not five

`.curvePerReel([...])` - a camera each. Bend the middle reels hardest, ease off outward, and the board reads as one cylinder. Length must equal `reels()`; entries take the same shorthand or object form as `.curve()`.

## Tuning it live

`reelSet.setCurve()` re-projects at runtime - same value or a per-reel array. Dial it against your art instead of rebuilding:

```ts
reelSet.setCurve(0.4);
reelSet.setCurve([0.2, 0.35, 0.5, 0.35, 0.2]);
reelSet.setCurve(0); // flat
```

## On real art

Grid tiles are a measuring instrument. This is the same curve on shipped symbols - Playson's Supercharged Diamonds 3, the sheet the sprite Hold &amp; Win recipes use - at `curveFocus('set-lean')`.

These are trimmed atlas frames. Under `'symbol'` they could not keystone at all; under `'warp'` they bend like everything else.

Blur-on-spin is unaffected: motion-blur frames on `onReelSpinStart`, back on landing.

## Numbers, not art

One reel, three cells, money amounts spinning past - the pattern behind every prize picker, jackpot ladder and bonus readout. The symbols are drawn at runtime, and the symbol id IS the amount, so `setResult([['100.00', ...]])` lands the value you name.

Each cell is a `Graphics` plate with a `PIXI.Text` on it - a composite, not a texture - so `'symbol'` mode could only displace and scale it. Under the warp the plate and the digits bend together.

Text is authored at a large size and scaled down to fit, so `$1000.00` and `$5.00` sit at the same optical weight without reflowing the font.

## Extending `'symbol'` mode

Only relevant if you skip the warp. The engine hands each symbol a **projected quad** via `ReelSymbol.applyCellQuad()`:

- **`SpriteSymbol` / `AnimatedSpriteSymbol`** map it onto a PixiJS `PerspectiveMesh` - true keystone, no extra render pass - when the texture owns its whole source. `canProjectTexture()` refuses atlas sub-frames; the mesh addresses its source with 0..1 UVs and remapping them onto the frame has not produced a correct draw here.
- **Everything else** gets a *uniform* scale about the quad's centre, sized to fit INSIDE it so no cell overlaps its neighbour. Uniform on purpose: squashing one axis turns a `7` into a squashed `7`, not a `7` at an angle.

Own symbol, single texture? Write `applyCellQuad()`. `PerspectiveCell` is exported and does the mesh plumbing.

**Art that does not fill its cell** - most does not - reports its real rect via `ReelSymbol.cellInset`. Sprite symbols derive it from the texture's trim, so a TexturePacker atlas needs no help. Without it the drum projects the empty cell instead of the gem inside it.

**Gotcha:** the affine fallback owns `view.scale`. A win animation tweening `symbol.view.scale` to an absolute value overwrites the projection while it runs. Animate a child of `view` instead. Not an issue under `'warp'`.

## The engine does not notice

The strip still marches on flat coordinates. The projection never touches a view's `position`, so `setResult()`, wrapping, cascades, big symbols and MultiWays reshapes behave exactly as flat.

`getCellBounds()` returns the projected cell's **bounding box**; `getCellQuad()` returns the four corners it is actually drawn on. Outline with the quad, hit-test with the box.

**Gotcha:** curvature is presentation only. It never changes what lands where, what `getVisibleGrid()` returns, or any event payload.
