## One number

A slot reel is a drum. The middle cell faces you; the outer ones have rotated away, so their far edge sits further from your eye. A camera turns those into **trapezoids**, not smaller rectangles.

`.curve(amount)`. `0` flat (default, free), `1` hard barrel. Most boards want `0.3`&ndash;`0.5`.

The grid on the tiles is the tell: lines converge on the outer rows, stay parallel on the middle one.

<RecipeDemo code="curved-reels" height={420} />

`depth` is perspective strength - how much smaller an edge cell is than the middle. `0` gives an orthographic drum: cells bunch, nothing recedes, nothing keystones. Defaults to `amount * 0.5`, capped just under `cos(arc)` where the projection would fold cells back over each other.

```ts
builder.curve(0.45);                          // depth 0.225
builder.curve({ amount: 0.45, depth: 0.35 }); // stronger
```

## The middle cell is never deformed

The cell facing the camera is drawn at authored size, both axes, no keystone. Everything bends around it.

So it cannot also fill a window sized for N flat cells. The ends fall short, and the buffer cells show in that band, compressed as they curve away. Frame it - that band is the sliver of the next symbol a real cabinet shows, and a real cabinet's bezel covers it. `bufferSymbols(2)` gives it more to draw.

Stretching to the window edges instead magnifies the main axis but not the cross axis: a visibly taller middle row.

## Two ways to draw it

Every demo here uses `.curveMode('warp')`.

```ts
builder.curve(0.45).curveMode('warp').renderer(app.renderer);
```

**`'warp'`** renders each reel to a texture and displaces the **vertices** of a mesh. Everything inside bends - Spine, atlas sprites, text, effects - and no symbol cooperates or knows. It also bends **motion**: the curve is on the rendered reel, so the spin, stop bounce and cascade falls ride it.

**`'symbol'`** (default) projects each cell alone. Crisper, free, but a `Container` transform is affine, so it only bends content that IS a texture. Spine, `Graphics`, composites and *atlas sub-frames* get a uniform scale instead: displaced and shrunk, never keystoned. The stop bounce translates the reel flat, so the board slides rather than travelling round the drum.

| | `'symbol'` | `'warp'` |
|---|---|---|
| Spine / composites bend | no | **yes** |
| Atlas art keystones | no | **yes** |
| Motion follows the curve | no | **yes** |
| Cost | free | 1 render pass per reel per frame |
| Sharpness | native | one resample |
| Needs `renderer()` | no | **yes** - `build()` throws without it |

Under `'warp'`, symbols are not real display objects to the renderer: use `getCellQuad()`, not screen-space hit testing.

**Gotcha:** anything the engine LIFTS out of a reel is not in that reel's texture, so `'warp'` cannot bend it - `unmask: true` symbols, the win spotlight, pin flights and overlays all draw flat over a curved board. The builder logs when it sees `unmask` under `'warp'`. `'symbol'` has no such split.

### Symbols that hang over their cell

`SymbolData.zIndex` stacks within a reel, folded in as `zIndex * 100 + cellIndex`, so any raised value beats every cell index. Reapplied on every wrap, so it holds mid-spin.

`.curveBleed(px)` gives the warp texture room across the strip. Art wider than its cell is otherwise sliced at the texture edge; bleed captures the overhang and warps it with everything else.

```ts
builder
  .symbolData({ mystery: { zIndex: 2 } }) // draws over its neighbours
  .curveBleed(46);                        // and may hang over them
```

Pair bleed with `SharedRectMaskStrategy` - any `curveFocus` other than `'reel'` selects it - or the per-reel mask clips the overhang back off.

## Where the camera stands

Default: one camera per reel, each its own drum. `.curveFocus('set')`: one camera on the middle of the board, so receding cells lean IN and the grid reads as one wide cylinder.

```ts
builder.curve(0.5).curveFocus('reel');     // default - five separate drums
builder.curve(0.5).curveFocus('set-lean'); // halfway
builder.curve(0.5).curveFocus('set');      // one camera, whole board
```

Outer reels tilt toward the middle; the centre one barely moves. `'set-lean'` is usually the sweet spot on a 5-wide board.

<RecipeDemo code="curved-reels-focus" height={420} />

**Gotcha:** leaning cells cross their own column, and the per-reel `RectMaskStrategy` would slice the overhang. Anything but `'reel'` auto-selects `SharedRectMaskStrategy`; an explicit `.maskStrategy(...)` still wins.

## A pay band inside a taller drum

Five cells drawn, three paying. The outer rows exist to be curved: they are the part of the drum turning away, and they are what makes it read as a cylinder rather than a grid. They land real symbols - the strip does not know they are decorative - the game evaluates rows 1-3.

Better for a curve than a bare 3x5, where the drum must bend hard enough to distort the symbols people are reading. Here the bend is spent on rows nobody reads and the band sits near 1:1, so it takes a deeper `curve(0.62)`.

The scrim comes from `getCellQuad()`, not `getCellBounds()`, whose bounding box is looser than the cell and would spill onto neighbours.

## Cascades on a drum

The case the warp exists for. The bend is on the rendered reel, so everything the tumble moves rides the curve: symbols exploding out, survivors sliding down, fresh ones dropping in. Nothing in the cascade code knows.

**A tumble board must frame the band.** Elsewhere it is a nicety; here it is a defect twice over. At the top it shows the buffer cell - the refill queue - which does not slide when survivors fall, so it sits frozen while the grid drops past. At the bottom there is nothing to show, because a pure tumble sets `bufferSymbols({ end: 0 })`. A bezel covers both; `curve.mapMain(0)` gives its depth.

**Gotcha worth copying:** the flat `cascade-6x5-spine` recipe puts `unmask: true` on its high symbol to overflow the mask. Under the warp that lifts it out of the reel texture and it draws flat. Use `curveBleed` instead.

## Tuning it live

`reelSet.setCurve()` re-projects at runtime - same value or a per-reel array:

```ts
reelSet.setCurve(0.4);
reelSet.setCurve([0.2, 0.35, 0.5, 0.35, 0.2]);
reelSet.setCurve(0); // flat
```

## On real art

Grid tiles are a measuring instrument. Same curve on shipped symbols - Playson's Supercharged Diamonds 3, the sheet the sprite Hold &amp; Win recipes use - at `curveFocus('set-lean')`.

Trimmed atlas frames. Under `'symbol'` they could not keystone at all; under `'warp'` they bend like everything else. Blur-on-spin is unaffected.

## Numbers, not art

One reel, three cells, money amounts - the pattern behind prize pickers and jackpot ladders. Symbols are drawn at runtime and the symbol id IS the amount, so `setResult([['100.00', ...]])` lands the value you name.

Each cell is a `PIXI.Text`, a composite with no texture of its own, so `'symbol'` could only displace and scale it. Under the warp the digits themselves bend.

## Extending `'symbol'` mode

Only relevant if you skip the warp. The engine hands each symbol a projected quad via `ReelSymbol.applyCellQuad()`:

- **`SpriteSymbol` / `AnimatedSpriteSymbol`** map it onto a `PerspectiveMesh` - true keystone, no extra render pass - when the texture owns its whole source. `canProjectTexture()` refuses atlas sub-frames: the mesh addresses its source with 0..1 UVs and remapping them onto the frame has not produced a correct draw here.
- **Everything else** gets a uniform scale about the quad's centre, sized to fit INSIDE it so no cell overlaps its neighbour. Uniform on purpose: squashing one axis turns a `7` into a squashed `7`, not a `7` at an angle.

Own symbol, single texture? Write `applyCellQuad()`. `PerspectiveCell` is exported and does the mesh plumbing.

**Art that does not fill its cell** - most does not - reports its real rect via `ReelSymbol.cellInset`. Sprite symbols derive it from the texture's trim. Without it the drum projects the empty cell instead of the gem inside it.

**Gotcha:** the affine fallback owns `view.scale`. A win animation tweening `symbol.view.scale` to an absolute value overwrites the projection while it runs. Animate a child of `view` instead. Not an issue under `'warp'`.

## The engine does not notice

The strip still marches on flat coordinates. The projection never touches a view's `position`, so `setResult()`, wrapping, cascades, big symbols and MultiWays reshapes behave exactly as flat.

`getCellBounds()` returns the projected cell's **bounding box**; `getCellQuad()` returns the four corners it is drawn on. Outline with the quad, hit-test with the box.

**Gotcha:** curvature is presentation only. It never changes what lands where, what `getVisibleGrid()` returns, or any event payload.
