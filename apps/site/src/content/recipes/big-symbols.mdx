The first half of this page covers big symbols, one symbol that covers a block of cells. It starts with blocks that land in full view and moves on to blocks that land partly hidden in a buffer or scroll past during the spin. The second half covers boards whose reels show different numbers of cells: a fixed pyramid and MultiWays.

### Horizontal reels

A 2x3 block on a set built with `.orientation('horizontal')`, outlined on landing with `getBlockBounds`. `size: { reels, cells }` is in index space and does not rotate. `reels` always counts across reels, and `cells` always counts along the strip.

The screen shape flips with the orientation. `{ reels: 2, cells: 3 }` is 2 wide and 3 tall on a vertical set, and 2 tall and 3 wide here. `getBlockBounds` works in screen space, so its rect flips too.

## Blocks in the buffer

A block's anchor can sit outside the visible window, so only part of the block shows when the reel lands. The game then brings the rest into view.

<a id="big-symbol-partial-land"></a>

### Land partly in the buffer

A 1x3 block lands with its anchor in `bufferStart`, so only its bottom cell shows, at cell 0. A downward nudge drags the rest into view.

<RecipeDemo code="big-symbol-partial-land" height={300} />

<a id="big-symbol-held-respin"></a>

### Nudge in, then hold for a respin

The partly hidden wild is nudged into full view. Then the other reels respin with `holdReels`, so the block stays where it is.

<RecipeDemo code="big-symbol-held-respin" height={300} />

<a id="big-symbols-reverse-tail"></a>

### Reverse reels

The same partial landing on a reel built with `.direction('reverse')`, which rolls up. The block is anchored at the last visible cell with its body in `bufferEnd`, and a `'forward'` nudge, which moves up on this reel, drags it into full view.

Buffers are positional and do not follow the travel direction. `bufferStart` is above and `bufferEnd` is below in both directions, so a reel that feeds from the bottom shows its partial block out of `bufferEnd`.

<RecipeDemo code="big-symbols-reverse-tail" height={300} />

<a id="big-symbol-cascade-fall"></a>

### Fall into view in a cascade

A tall wild lands with its anchor in `bufferStart` and only its tail showing. The cells below it win, and the wild falls into full view as they clear. The second demo does the same with a 2x2 block across two reels.

The anchor's fall distance must equal the number of winning cells cleared below it, or the block snaps instead of falling.

## Blocks during the spin

<a id="tall-symbols-in-spin"></a>

### Tall blocks in the spin

A 1x3 block with a weight scrolls past on reel 0 while it spins, and a scatter only ever scrolls past on reel 4. Both weights are set in per-reel pools: `0` in the base `weights()` table, and raised on the one reel that may show the symbol. Only blocks one reel wide can have a weight. Wider blocks still appear only on landing, because the random fill feeds one reel at a time.

A narrower pool cannot lift an `exclude` set by a wider one, but it can raise a weight of `0`. To allow a symbol on one reel only, use weights, not `exclude`. A weighted block also needs `bufferSymbols({ start: size.cells })`, and the builder checks for it. The block enters a forward reel bottom first and leaves a reverse reel anchor first, and the buffer slots hold its covered cells while the anchor that draws them is off the strip.

<RecipeDemo code="tall-symbols-in-spin" height={340} />

<a id="tall-peek"></a>

### Peek, then bring it up

Two scatters slow reel 4 into an anticipation crawl, and the weighted 1x3 keeps sliding past during it. The result lands the block with its head on the last visible cell and its body in `bufferEnd`, so the set uses `bufferSymbols({ start: 3, end: 2 })`: `start` for the weight and `end` for the peek. The two demos then bring the block up in different ways.

- Nudge up: `nudge(reel, { distance: 2, direction: 'reverse' })` moves the whole column, and the two cards above scroll out through the top.
- Expand up: the landed anchor is already one sprite three cells tall, with its body under the mask. Tween its view up two cells so it grows over the cards, then call `setColumn(reel, { visible: ['tall', ...] })` to commit the block at the top of the column. The rest of the board stays put. In engine terms this is an expanding wild.

`getBlockBounds` reports the whole block even while part of it is in a buffer. The mask does the clipping.

### MultiWays

Each reel lands on a different cell count every spin, and the number of ways is the product of the cell counts.

Call `setShape(cellsPerReel)` before `setResult()` on every spin.

### MultiWays cascade

A MultiWays shape on a normal strip landing, followed by a cascade that removes the ways winners and drops new symbols in from above.

Call `setShape` before `setResult`, and start the round with `spin({ mode: 'standard' })` so `AdjustPhase` reshapes the reels between the spin and stop phases.

### Sticky wild on MultiWays

A pin keeps its place across reshapes through its remembered `originCell`, with the default `migration: 'origin'`. It moves in when the reel shrinks and returns when the reel grows back. With `'frozen'`, each clamp rewrites the origin, so the pin never moves back.
