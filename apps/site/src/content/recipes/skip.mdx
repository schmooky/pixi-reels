A slam force-completes every active phase, `AnticipationPhase` included. That is the right default and the wrong one: it makes the button honest on an ordinary spin and makes it hide the build-up on the spin that had one. These recipes take skip apart into the three levers that decide WHICH reels a press lands, and WHEN a reel is allowed to stop at all.

<a id="skip-partial-slam"></a>

## Land some reels, not all

`slamStop({ except: [3, 4] })` places reels 0-2 on the result this instant and leaves 3 and 4 running their own phase chains to a natural stop. `{ reels: [...] }` is the complement, for when it is easier to name the reels that should land.

**Gotcha:** a partial slam leaves `skipStage` alone and does not end the round, so the button stays live. Only a full slam moves the stage to `2` and applies the round's speed boost.

<a id="skip-reels-complement"></a>

## Name the reels that land

`slamStop({ reels })` is the complement: pass the reels to land instead of the reels to spare. Same lever, two spellings - reach for `reels` when the landing set is the short one or comes from game state, `except` when the spared set is. Here a three-beat plan walks the board outside in, with no tease and no protection involved.

**Gotcha:** neither form needs guarding. Out-of-range indices are ignored, and held or already-landed reels are never slammed.

<a id="skip-tease-protect"></a>

## A tease a press cannot hide

The same partial slam, expressed as a rule the engine applies for you. `protect: 'once'` makes the first press land every non-tease reel and leave the tease running, so the trigger symbols are on screen and the build-up is visibly under way; the next press ends it. `'always'` never lets a press end a tease.

**Gotcha:** call `setAnticipation` BEFORE `setResult`. A press in the pre-result window is queued by `requestSkip()` and fires the instant the result arrives, so the tease has to be registered by then or there is nothing to protect.

<a id="skip-stepwise-tease"></a>

## Walk the tease forward, one reel per press

`protect: 'stepwise'` is the same rule applied per reel. Press 1 lands everything outside the tease, and each press after that releases the next tease reel in tease order. The player sets the pace: mash through it, or stop pressing and let it play. The press that releases the last tease reel is the one that ends the round.

**Gotcha:** release order is TEASE order, not reel index. `setAnticipation([4, 2, 3], ...)` releases 4 first, matching the order you staged the teases in.

<RecipeDemo code="skip-stepwise-tease" />

For any other grouping - release the tease in pairs, or from the outside in - use `protect: 'always'` so a press can never end a tease, and drive the releases yourself with `slamStop({ reels })`:

```ts
reelSet.setAnticipation(tease, { protect: 'always' });

const queue = [[3, 4], [2]];   // your plan, in press order
function onSkipPressed() {
  const group = queue.shift();
  if (group) reelSet.slamStop({ reels: group });
}
```

**Gotcha:** two things that look like they would work here do not, and both are pinned by tests. Shrinking the set with `setAnticipation()` mid-spin does NOT release a reel that is already teasing - the anticipation set is read once, at the moment a reel leaves `SPIN`. And `setSpeed()` mid-spin does not re-time the round in flight: `spin()` captures the active profile and hands that instance to every phase, which is why the `skipSpin()` boost lands on the NEXT spin. `slamStop({ reels })` is the lever that works mid-tease.

<a id="skip-always-protect"></a>

## A tease no press can end

`protect: 'always'`. The first press lands everything outside the tease, exactly like `'once'`; every press after that is a deliberate no-op. Use it when the tease IS the reward beat and cutting it costs more than the impatience does - a jackpot reveal, a final-reel bonus land.

**Gotcha:** a refused press emits no skip events at all, rather than throwing. And `slamStop()` still lands everything: the engine reserves an unconditional exit for your own code (an abort, a timeout, a disconnect), it only refuses the player's press.

<a id="skip-queued-request"></a>

## Pressing before the server answers

`skipSpin()` throws before `setResult()` arrives - there is nothing to land on, and slamming would put the reels on random buffer fill. `requestSkip()` queues the intent and fires it the instant the result lands, with tease protection still applied. The demo's result is deliberately slow so the window is easy to hit.

**Gotcha:** this is why `setAnticipation` must come BEFORE `setResult`. A press queued in that window can only protect a tease the engine already knows about.

<a id="skip-stage-button"></a>

## Driving the button from skipStage

`0` no press yet, `1` a press landed the reels around a protected tease and left it running, `2` the round-ending press happened. Stage `1` is only reachable on a spin that asked for protection, so a game with no protected teases sees the old two-state button and nothing changes. The label here is read off `reelSet.skipStage` every frame rather than counted locally.

<a id="skip-turbo-inert"></a>

## No hold, no tease, nothing to protect

Turbo and SuperTurbo set `anticipationDelay: 0`. With an effective hold of `0` ms `protect` does nothing and every press lands everything - protecting a tease that never plays would stall the reels for nothing and put back the response-time tell. Spins here alternate on the same turbo profile with the same `protect`; only `duration` differs.

<a id="skip-no-tell"></a>

## Skip without a tell

The reason to protect the tease rather than slow the skip down. Spins alternate scatterless and teasing, both auto-press at the same moment, and the panel times how long the last non-tease reel takes to rest. The numbers stay equal: the press answers identically, and what differs happens after it, on screen.

Keeping the tease skippable and raising `minimumSpinTime` so a teasing spin cannot land instantly gets the pacing right and leaks the outcome through the response time itself. The player learns a feature is coming before a single reel has landed, from how the button felt.

<a id="skip-min-spin-time"></a>

## Per-reel spin floor

No slam here at all. `minimumSpinTime` lives on the speed profile, so it is one value shared by every reel, and `setStopDelays()` only re-orders stops on top of it. The two levers missed each other: instant was only ever global, and per-reel could not go below the floor. `setMinimumSpinTime([0, 0, 0, 1000, 1000])` overrides it per reel, so reels 0-2 take the result the moment it arrives while 3 and 4 are held a full second.

**Gotcha:** it persists across `spin()` and `refill()` until cleared with `null`, exactly like `setStopDelays()`. Set it once at boot, not per spin, unless you mean it to change.

<RecipeDemo code="skip-min-spin-time" />

<a id="skip-min-spin-uniform"></a>

## Uniform floor, and clearing it

A single number raises the floor on every reel; `null` clears the override and hands the floor back to the speed profile (which is not the same as a floor of `0`). Spins alternate between the two so the difference reads back to back.

<a id="skip-cascade-protect"></a>

## Cascades

Anticipation runs before the tumble/standard split in the phase chain, so a cascade spin teases and protects exactly like a strip spin. Three things differ, all about what a press carries rather than what it lands:

| | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Initial spin | `protect` applies unchanged. Press 1 lands the reels outside the tease, press 2 ends it. |
| Round side effect | In cascade mode this is auto-slam-refills, not a speed boost. A protected press does NOT spend it; the press that ends the tease does, and every refill after that lands instantly. |
| Refills | Carry no tease. `refill()` clears the anticipation set on entry, so a press inside one is an ordinary full slam - though `slamStop({ reels })` still works per reel there. |

**Gotcha:** `refill()` and `runCascade()` listen to `skip:requested` only to set `wasSkipped` on their result. A partial slam does not abort the chain, but it does mark the whole result `wasSkipped: true`.

<a id="skip-hold-and-win"></a>

## Hold & Win

Nothing to demo here, and that is the point: a Hold & Win board is one single-reel `ReelSet` **per cell**, not one set with many reels. Per-reel slam has no granularity to add, because `slamStop({ reels })` and `{ except }` can only ever name reel `0`. The granularity you want is per CELL, and it already exists as `BoardGrid.skipSpinning()` / `HoldAndWinBoard.skip()`, which press each cell's set individually.

Tease protection on a cell's set behaves consistently but rarely usefully, since "every reel outside the tease" is the empty set:

- `'once'` - press 1 lands nothing and spends the protection, press 2 lands the cell. That is protection working, not a bug: the impatient first press is spent making the tease visible.
- `'stepwise'` - the release group IS the tease reel, so one press lands it.
- `'always'` - **do not use it on a Hold & Win cell.** No press can ever land that cell, and `BoardGrid.skipSpinning()`'s error-recovery path needs it to land or the next `respin()` throws "already spinning" on it. `slamStop()` still lands it, which is the escape hatch that path would need.

<a id="phase-subclass-stop"></a>

## Subclass a built-in phase

Every built-in phase class is exported, so a small change is a subclass rather than a rewrite. `StopPhase` here gains a landing backlight; the stop sequencer, the bounce and the skip pose are inherited untouched. Register under the same key (`'start'`, `'spin'`, `'anticipation'`, `'stop'`) to replace the built-in.

**Gotcha:** call `super` from every hook you override. `onEnter` starts the real spin-out, and a reel whose `onEnter` never reaches it will spin forever. Override `onSkip` too when the beat should also fire on a slammed reel.

<a id="phase-subclass-spin"></a>

## A spin floor the phase decides for itself

`SpinPhaseConfig.minimumSpinTime` documented an override of the profile's shared floor that could not be reached, because registering through `PhaseFactory` meant writing a spin phase from scratch. Subclass `SpinPhase` and it is one line. `setMinimumSpinTime([...])` is the ready-made version; reach for the subclass when the floor is derived rather than configured.

**Gotcha:** merge the config, don't replace it - `super.onEnter({ ...config, minimumSpinTime })`. The controller may already be passing a floor, and clobbering the object drops anything added to it later.

<a id="phase-subclass-anticipation"></a>

## Using update() from a subclass

A per-reel countdown drawn from inside the tease, with each hook doing its job: `onEnter` shows the label then `super` starts the slow-down, `update(deltaMs)` runs every frame while active, `onSkip` takes the label off when a press force-completes the phase.

**Gotcha:** `update` is ticker-driven accumulation, not wall clock, which is why it stays correct in a backgrounded tab where `performance.now()` would not. And a phase is handed the active `SpeedProfile` as `this._speed`, so a subclass never has to be told the timings the engine is already using.

<a id="phase-from-scratch-start"></a>

## A phase written from scratch

Extend `ReelPhase` when you want the whole behaviour rather than a tweak - here a flat linear ramp with no step-back pull, replacing `StartPhase` under its own key.

**Gotcha:** a custom NAME buys nothing on its own. The controller only ever asks the factory for the lifecycle keys, so `register('myPhase', ...)` leaves it orphaned - nothing constructs it. Own a phase by taking its key. And call `this._complete()` exactly once, or the reel never advances and the spin promise never settles.

<a id="phase-subclass-cascade"></a>

## Phases that take constructor arguments

The three cascade phases and MultiWays' `AdjustPhase` carry build-time config as extra constructor arguments, so a subclass needs `registerFactory` and has to forward them. `resolveTumbleConfig(config)` produces the shape they expect - the same call `.tumble()` makes internally.

**Gotcha:** note where `.phases(...)` sits in this recipe - before `.tumble(...)`, and it still wins. Configurators are deferred to `build()` and applied after the built-in registrations. Before 2.3 they ran at call time while `.tumble()` registered its defaults inside `build()`, so a `'cascade:*'` override was silently discarded wherever you put it.
